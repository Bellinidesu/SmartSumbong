# Villamor Air Base: what to keep, what to remove

The City view is built zone by zone, and by removing: the zone starts from everything the pipeline draws there now, and this list says what goes. Numbers are for Ace to pick ("remove 1, 3, 4").
Counts are from the current build (10 Oct 2026): the Villamor Air Base polygon (OSM way 43762468, about 61 ha) holds **465 buildings** (411 of them untagged), 10 trees, 12 street lamps.

## What is there (what is known, kept short)
- Villamor Air Base is the headquarters of the Philippine Air Force. Named for Captain Jesus Villamor (since 1948); before that Nichols Field. It shares its fence with NAIA's runways.
- On the map: **Philippine Air Force Headquarters** (31 m in our data, an estimate), **PAF Headquarters Grandstand**, **New Grandstand Building**, **Bulwagang Katatagan** (function hall), **Lakambini Hall**,
  **Gate 5 Officers Club House**, **Villamor VIP Lounge**, **Service Support Group HQ**, **950th Communication Center and Air Defense Wing**, **505th Air Rescue Group**, **207th Tactical Helicopter Squadron Hangar**,
  **1st Air Reserve Center HQ**, three **Philippine Air Force Hangars** (4,000 to 6,000 m2 each), **Airmen's Mall**, **Villamor Post Exchange**, **24/7 Convenience Store**, **Petron**, **Multilevel Parking Space**,
  **Ecumenical Chapel**, **Our Lady of Loreto Chapel**, the **Air Force Aerospace Museum** with its **Retired Aircraft Garden**, the **Villamor Air Base Golf Course**, and the housing blocks round them.
- Staying detailed by the rule of 9 Oct: the Aerospace Museum complex, its Retired Aircraft Garden, the golf course (the green of the area).

## Remove (proposed)
1. **House detail on the 411 untagged buildings**: doors, windows, air-conditioners, dishes, water tanks, stair heads, chimneys, awnings, rim lines. Air-base quarters are plain blocks; they go back to footprint, height, colour.
2. **Cars** parked and driving on the base's streets (the base is a closed site; cars here are guesses).
3. **Balconies, solar panels and antenna masts** on the base's mid-rise buildings (invented).
4. **Parked airliners** on any apron or stand that lies inside the Villamor polygon (planes stay at NAIA Terminal 3's own stands only; the museum's garden aircraft stay).
5. **Runway and taxiway markings** inside the Villamor polygon, except where it joins the NAIA airfield (keep the pavement, drop centre lines, edge lights, rubber).
6. **Entrances, flagpoles and porches** added by rule on the base's civic, school and mall models (the porch on the chapels stays).
7. **Street lamps** inside the polygon (12): the base is lit by its own, and they float beside buildings that are plain anyway.

## Fix, not remove (typing mistakes found)
8. **1st Air Reserve Center HQ** and the **Aerospace Museum** are typed *hotel* (and get hotel ribs and a podium ledge): make them *civic*.
9. **Hangars by name**: the three Philippine Air Force Hangars, the 207th Helicopter Squadron Hangar and the 505th Air Rescue Group are typed from nothing and stay plain boxes: type them *hangar* (a hangar is a hangar).
10. **Philippine Air Force Headquarters, 31 m**: the stand-in height. The atlas has no better answer for it; set about 18 m (five floors) unless you know the floor count.

## Keep
Footprints and real heights; the plain colour by type; Aerospace Museum, Retired Aircraft Garden, golf course as detailed; the NAIA Terminal 3 side of the airfield.
